AI & Future of Work: Student Feature Guide

futureofjobs.vercel.app · Data: BLS EP & OEWS, O*NET 30.1, Anthropic Claude
This guide explains every feature of the AI & Future of Work visualisation tool. It covers all 830 BLS occupations, in every field, to help you analyse how artificial intelligence is reshaping work, so you can make strategic decisions about your career or studies.
01🏔️

3D Terrain Map

The map is one landscape split into 22 fields: the BLS major occupation groups, from Arts & Media to Healthcare and Construction. A field's area is its share of all U.S. workers in 2025 (BLS Employment Projections, self-employed included). The fields are grouped by the five official BLS job families, outlined on the map: area is workers, while positions within a family follow BLS code order, so where a field sits and its shape are only layout. Inside each field, related occupations sit together: each BLS minor group has its own block, in code order. Each peak is one occupation, and its height is its number of jobs from BLS Employment Projections. Open a family or a field and the map zooms into it: the same block, magnified, with every occupation next to the same neighbours (inside a field it is also drawn closer to a square so its labels can be read). What you have open is drawn on one fixed scale: the largest job count any of its occupations reaches from 2025 to 2035 is full height. Each hill rises or falls with its own BLS number as you move the slider, though a small hill on a larger neighbour's slope can move with that neighbour. Compare heights within what you have open.

Student tip: Click a job family to zoom into it, then a field to see its occupations, or pick any field from the All 22 fields button. The largest are labelled; hover or tap any other peak for its name. Back, Esc or the trail next to that button takes you back up, and the line under it gives the numbers for what you have open.
02⏱️

AI Timeline Slider

Drag the slider from 2025 → 2035 to watch each occupation's BLS Employment Projections job count year by year. Height moves in a straight line between the official 2025 and 2035 employment levels — no AI forecast on the landscape.

Student tip: Compare Registered Nurses with Retail Salespersons at 2035. Notice which peak grows and which shrinks, then open both to compare.
03🎓

Fields & Kelley Roles

AI scores cover 783 of the 830 occupations: the 50 Kelley business roles (47 BLS occupations) plus 736 more. They are drawn in their risk colours. The occupations outside the 50 are scored the same way but are not Kelley roles. Slate peaks are the 47 “All Other” groups for which O*NET lists no tasks, so they are not scored. Nothing is shown in place of a missing score.

Student tip: Search any job title in the header: all 830 BLS occupations are there, from Actors to Electricians.
04📋

Job Detail Panel

Inside a field, click any peak to open its panel: what the job involves (O*NET), how many people do it (BLS Employment Projections, 2025, self-employed included), median pay (BLS OEWS, payroll employees only), the BLS 2025–35 growth outlook, and its day-to-day O*NET tasks. For the 50 Kelley roles the panel also shows Automation Risk, Human Criticality and Human Resilience, with a risk score on every task. Automation Risk is the plain average of those task scores, so you can check it yourself. Every other scored occupation shows the same scores on its five rated O*NET tasks, and says where it would sit among the 50 Kelley roles.

Student tip: Two worker counts are normal: pay comes from payroll employees only, while "workers" counts everyone, self-employed included. Scores measure GenAI capability, not guaranteed adoption.
05🧩

Tasks View

Open Tasks in the header to see each Kelley role broken into AI-capable vs human-needed work (other occupations are not in it yet). Roles are ordered by average GenAI capability today — not guaranteed adoption. Use “Same score, different jobs” pairs to make the contrast concrete.

Student tip: Capability labels are Claude judgments of what GenAI can handle, not a forecast that the work is already automated.
06🤖

AI Analysis

Open any of the 50 Kelley roles and Claude explains its published scores right in the panel: the reasoning under each task, a strategic insight for the role, and the likely replacements and human traits it calls for. The analysis adds the explanation; it never changes the numbers. The scored occupations outside the 50 show their published scores with no live analysis.

Student tip: Every student sees the same published scores for a role, dated in the header badge, so you can compare notes. The analysis is where you get the why behind them.
07🔮

Scenario Planning

On a Kelley role, the Scenario button asks "what if": what happens to this role if AI adoption accelerates, if regulation tightens, or if economic conditions shift? It generates a written strategic brief.

Student tip: Use this to stress-test career choices. Ask the same role under two different scenarios and compare the outlooks.
08🗺️

Career Roadmap

On a Kelley role, identifies the highest-risk task and builds a personalised 6-month plan to defend it — own the judgment and oversight around the automatable work — while deepening a human-critical task. Covers skills, tools, and learning resources.

Student tip: Open it with the Roadmap button on the role panel. Defend = supervise the high-risk task; Build = deepen the human-critical one.
09🎯

My Skills (CV Match)

Paste your CV or a list of your skills. The app analyses which roles align with your strengths and flags where you are most exposed to automation risk.

Student tip: Honest input = honest output. List the skills you actually have, not the ones you wish you had.
10🗺️

US Map View

Switch from the 3D terrain to a flat 2D US map of the 50 Kelley roles (state data for other occupations is not in the app yet). State colour and circle size both encode BLS employment volume: darker and larger means more workers in that state for the selected roles. Hover a state to see the exact BLS OEWS May 2025 employment count and Location Quotient (LQ) per occupation.

Student tip: Select specific roles using the role filter before opening the map to compare which states are most exposed for those jobs in particular.
11📊

Job Security Index

The colour legend at the bottom right of the screen maps the scored roles into three bands: High Automation Risk (red), Hybrid/Augmented (amber), and Safe Human-Centric (green). Bands are relative, graded on a curve across the 50 Kelley roles; every other scored occupation is placed on that same curve. Slate peaks are the 47 “All Other” groups for which O*NET lists no tasks, so they are not scored.

Student tip: A "green" role here means it is safer than most others in this dataset, not that it is completely immune to automation.
12📚

Data Sources

Workers in each occupation and each field come from the BLS Employment Projections 2025–35, which count everyone, self-employed included; growth outlooks come from the same projections. Pay comes from the BLS Occupational Employment & Wage Statistics (OEWS, May 2025), which covers payroll employees only. Task descriptions are from O*NET 30.1. AI risk scores are Claude's ratings of each O*NET task for the 50 Kelley roles and 736 other occupations, published with the app, so every student sees the same numbers. Each occupation is scored on five of its O*NET tasks: its own first five, or, where O*NET splits the job into smaller codes, one task from each in turn. The other 47 are “All Other” groups for which O*NET lists no tasks, so they are not scored.

Student tip: Click "Sources" at the bottom of the screen to read the full sourcing and calculation methodology.
